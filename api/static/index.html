How the number is worked out

For each player the model reads only matches played before the date being predicted, then sets the two against each other on four counts:

Recent form
Win rate over the last ten and last five matches, days since they last played, and how busy the past fortnight has been.
Opponent quality
Wins weighted by who they came against — beating a top-ten player counts roughly six times a win over someone outside the top 250, and losing to a low-ranked player costs more than losing to a seed. Thin records are pulled toward even, so a 3–0 run isn't read as unbeatable.
Surface
Win rate on this surface over the past year, and the opponent-quality version of the same figure.
Head-to-head and context
Previous meetings weighted by how recent they were, plus season win rate, ranking gap and retirement rate.

Those comparisons feed a gradient-boosted classifier trained on 24,000 ATP and WTA matches, then calibrated against real outcomes — a stated 60% means that player won about 60% of the time in testing.

Ranking is one input among eighteen, not the starting point. Sportsbook prices are never fed in as a feature; they're only used afterwards for comparison. Backtesting across 11,817 matches found the model well calibrated but unable to beat closing odds.
